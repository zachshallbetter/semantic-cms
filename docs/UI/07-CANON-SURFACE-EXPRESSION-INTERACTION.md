# Canon, Surface, Expression, Representation, Interaction

This decomposition is the conceptual backbone of the system.

## Canon — what exists

Canon owns stable semantic identity and state: records, revisions, relations, provenance, entitlement declarations, and other authoritative domain facts.

Canon does not own layout, current presentation, or transient observational state.

## Access — what may be known

Access projection narrows canonical state before semantic participation is resolved. Inaccessible state must not affect a viewer’s resulting surface when that state is outside the viewer’s observable scope.

## Surface — what matters here

A surface is a bounded contextual selection and organization of semantic state.

It can resolve:

- subject;
- purpose;
- context;
- lens;
- eligibility;
- membership;
- grouping;
- ordering;
- semantic priority;
- operation exposure;
- dependencies;
- explanation.

A surface does not own graphical morphology or rendering.

## Expression — how it may appear

Expression determines how semantic structure can be realized while preserving required meaning, operation identity, accessibility, and other protected invariants.

Expression may vary radically without changing the underlying surface.

## Representation — what is encountered

Representation is the actual delivered experience: visual, textual, spoken, machine-readable, printed, or another accessible form.

## Interaction — what may happen

Interaction governs consequential operations. Exposure of an operation does not imply permission, enablement, execution, or success.

Persistent mutation leaves the read/derivation path, crosses a governed interaction boundary, produces a receipt, and creates new canonical state.

## Complete loop

```text
CANON
what exists
   ↓
ACCESS
what may be known
   ↓
SURFACE
what matters here
   ↓
EXPRESSION
how it may appear
   ↓
REPRESENTATION
what is encountered
   ↓
INTERACTION
what may happen
   ↓
CONTRACT
what is authorized to change
   ↓
CANON
```
