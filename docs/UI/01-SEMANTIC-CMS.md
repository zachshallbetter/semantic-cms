# What Is a Semantic CMS?

A Semantic CMS is a content system in which the primary thing being managed is not a collection of pages, documents, or database rows. It manages a structured, evolving body of meaning.

A conventional CMS begins with artifacts such as pages, articles, images, categories, and navigation. A Semantic CMS begins one level beneath those artifacts: identity, content, meaning, structure, relationships, evidence, state, authority, provenance, operations, and observations.

Pages, feeds, search results, applications, notifications, agent context, APIs, and other experiences are derived from that semantic substrate.

## Core proposition

> Content is canonical semantic state. What people and machines encounter is a governed projection of that state.

## Canon instead of pages

At the foundation is a canonical record graph. An article, person, organization, image, concept, collection, relationship, entitlement, schema, or other meaningful object has stable identity and explicit structure.

Relationships are data, not merely hyperlinks. A record may be written by a person, reference another work, belong to a collection, contradict a claim, depend on a source, or participate in a larger concept neighborhood.

## Meaning and presentation are separate

A Semantic CMS does not define an article by its visual layout. It defines semantic participation such as title, introduction, primary media, body, supporting material, actions, and relationships.

Presentation is separate. One expression may be editorial, another dense and operational, another accessible and linear, another machine-readable. Protected meaning remains stable while expression varies.

## Surface resolution

The system does not expose the entire semantic graph every time. A semantic surface resolves a bounded question:

> What matters here, for this purpose, for this participant, at this time, under these access constraints?

Conceptually:

```text
Canonical state
      ↓
Access projection
      ↓
Semantic surface resolution
      ↓
Expression
      ↓
Representation
```

A surface may organize a subject into primary, supporting, contextual, contradictory, historical, evidentiary, unknown, or action-oriented participation. That result is still not a webpage. It is a renderer-neutral semantic surface.

## Context changes participation, not truth

The same canonical object can produce different valid surfaces under different purposes.

- Read: emphasize content and nearby context.
- Edit: emphasize editable structure, revisions, and obligations.
- Understand: emphasize relations, evidence, contradictions, and concepts.
- Qualify: emphasize required evidence and unresolved conditions.
- Monitor: emphasize delivery, freshness, drift, and propagation.

The object has not changed. The question being asked of the system has changed.

## Multidimensional state

A Semantic CMS does not collapse content into a single status field. A record can simultaneously be semantically complete, evidence-stale, promoted, and delivery-drifted. Those are different truths and remain separately inspectable.

Likewise, unknown, absent, hidden, unavailable, unsupported, and withheld are not interchangeable.

## Provenance

Information retains where it came from and what kind of claim it represents.

- Declared — explicitly asserted by the owning authority.
- Derived — mechanically produced from identified inputs.
- Observed — measured from a running system and time-bounded.
- System-certified — emitted by a trusted pipeline from validated inputs.

These classes do not silently promote into one another.

## Governed change

Reading and deriving are easy. Persistent change is explicit.

```text
Actor
  ↓
Operation
  ↓
Authority / validation
  ↓
Mutation
  ↓
New revision
  ↓
Receipt
```

The system can therefore explain who changed something, what changed, from which revision, under what authority, and with what resulting state.

## Publishing

Publishing is not equivalent to saving. A candidate may be complete and qualified without being promoted. Qualification asks whether the required evidence is sufficient; promotion asks whether the appropriate authority chooses to make the consequence real.

## Realtime

Realtime has three obligations:

- R1 — live co-authoring.
- R2 — live propagation.
- R3 — live honesty.

The third is the constraint: the system should accurately disclose what is actually current rather than merely appearing instant.

## Static content

Static content is not a separate subsystem. An About page or policy page is semantic content whose canonical state changes infrequently. Static describes delivery behavior, not a different kind of truth.

## Agents and AI

Agents consume bounded semantic surfaces with provenance and access already applied. They can propose candidates, but machine output does not become canonical merely because it was generated. Human and machine contributions participate in the same authority and provenance model.

## Short definition

> A Semantic CMS is a system for governing canonical meaning and deriving contextual experiences from it.
