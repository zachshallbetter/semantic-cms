# Document review and audit — 2026-08-29

**Scope:** every canonical document and record — `DESIGN.md`, `SPEC_HEALTH.md` and its claim
register, `work/GRAPH.md`, `records/*.jsonl`, the 45 work files, and the research documents.
**Method:** mechanical cross-reference sweep first, then each claim read against what the code
actually does today.

Eight findings. Four were mechanical and are fixed; three are process drift and one is a judgement
about how much retroactive record-keeping is honest.

---

## Clean

**No dangling references, anywhere.** 22 SH entries, 67 work items, 18 negative results, 58
evidence records, 3 blockers — every identifier referenced in any document resolves to a definition.
Every work file has a graph row. This is partly because `check-work-graph.py` landed hours ago and
partly luck; before that gate existed, the same sweep would have found the SCMS-044 collision and
the E13 epic that five items cited and nothing defined.

## Fixed in this pass

### 1. The composed-proof claim was stale by ten vectors
The register cited *"impl/e2e, 8 vectors"*. It is **18** — SCMS-027 and SCMS-049 each extended the
composed proof as the system grew. The claim was *understated*, which is the less dangerous
direction and still wrong: a register that lags reality in either direction stops being usable as a
reference.

### 2. The expression claim understated the system and overstated the instrument
It read *"two materially different ways… SCMS-009: 10 vectors"*. There are now **three** adapters,
and SCMS-037 held the third to the same contract rather than trusting it.

More importantly, the row didn't record that **the conformance checker itself was corrected twice**
in two days — NR-scms-011 (substring false positives) and NR-scms-013 (silent false negatives on
encoded identities). The instrument is younger than the claim it supports, and a reader deserved to
know that.

### 3. A blocker that outlived its reason — in the second register
`R1 live co-authoring` read *"blocked on P7/P22"*. P22 was accepted on 2026-08-28.

I corrected exactly this staleness in `work/GRAPH.md` earlier today and did not check whether the
same sentence appeared elsewhere. **Fixing an instance rather than a class** is the recurrence
NR-scms-011 warned about, committed again, in a register whose purpose is to prevent it.

### 4. One claim was materially overstated
*"Declared content types are enforced (where declared)"* was true of the contract runtime and false
of the system. NR-scms-016 found `governedImport` never supplied the validator, so **all 215 records
were created without their declared type ever being consulted**, and `ARTICLE_TYPE` did not declare
the `summary` slot the corpus actually carries. Fixed in SCMS-054; the row now says so.

### 5. Three capabilities had no claim at all
The Postgres store, content-addressed blobs, and materialization — three of the day's larger pieces —
were recorded in evidence and absent from the claim register. Added, each with what would strengthen
it. Notably all three carry the same caveat: **nothing in `impl/` reads or writes the store yet.**

## Process drift, recorded rather than papered over

### 6. The Ready-predicate discipline lapsed
**12 of 67 items are Done with no work file.** The early pattern — a work file with a full Ready
predicate, acceptance criteria, exclusions and stop conditions, written *before* implementation —
held for the first forty items and then thinned as the pace increased. SCMS-038, 039, 049, 052, 053,
055, 058, 060, 062 and others carry evidence records but never had a predicate.

**I am not writing those retroactively.** A Ready predicate's entire value is that it was written
before the work, when it could still constrain it; composing one afterwards from what happened
produces a document that looks like governance and functions as decoration. The honest record is
that the discipline lapsed for roughly a fifth of the items, and the evidence records carry the
substance in each case.

Going forward: a work file before implementation, or an explicit note in the graph row saying why
one was not warranted.

### 7. Three orphan evidence records
`scms-evidence-004a`, `-037a` and `-040a` are addenda that nothing references. Their content is real
— `-037a` records the write-boundary violation CI caught in the site server — but it is unreachable
from the graph, which cites the parent id. Addenda need a citation path or they are write-only.

### 8. Two registers, one truth, and no check between them
Findings 3 and 5 share a cause: `SPEC_HEALTH.md`'s claim register and `work/GRAPH.md` describe
overlapping reality with no mechanism relating them. `check-work-graph.py` validates the graph's
internal consistency and says nothing about whether the claim register agrees with it.

That is the next gate worth building, and it is not obvious how — "does this prose claim match that
work item's state" is not mechanically decidable in general. A narrow version is: **every claim row
that cites `SCMS-NNN` must cite an item whose state is consistent with the rung claimed.** That
would have caught finding 3.

## Not findings, but worth stating

**`DESIGN.md` did not require correction.** Every change tonight implemented it more faithfully
rather than departing from it — §13 already specified Postgres and append-only by grant, §8.3 already
resolved static against realtime, §8.5 already classified socket population as the bounded lane. The
design ran ahead of the implementation, and the audit found the implementation catching up to it
rather than the document drifting from the code.

**The claim ladder held.** Nothing was found sitting above its evidence except finding 4, and that
overstatement was introduced by an *inert hook* rather than by a wrong claim: the row described what
the write path did, and nobody checked that the import path used it.
